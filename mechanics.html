How it really works (from the game's code)

Everything on this page was read straight out of Megabonk's compiled code (v1.0.69, GameAssembly.dll decompiled with Il2CppDumper and a disassembler), not from testing or wikis. Where a step was only partly traced it says so.

1. How every stat is calculated

Final stat = (base + flat bonuses) × (1 + sum of all % bonuses) × (product of all multipliers)

  • "+X%" bonuses (most tomes, most items) add together inside one bracket. Ten +10% damage bonuses = ×2.0, not ×2.59.
  • "×X" multipliers multiply with each other and with the % bracket. That is why a single ×1.5 source is worth more the more % you already have.
  • Flat bonuses add to the base before anything is scaled.

Base values every character starts from (before character bonuses):

Three stats have diminishing returns built in:

  • Armor: real reduction = A / (A + 0.75), capped at 99.9%. Armor 0.25 = 25%, 0.75 = 50%, 1.5 = 67%, 3 = 80%.
  • Evasion: real chance = E / (E + 1), capped at 99.9%. Evasion 0.5 = 33%, 1.0 = 50%, 3.0 = 75%.
  • Max HP can't go below 1.

2. Weapon damage and crits

Hit damage = weapon damage × your Damage stat × crit multiplier × Crit damage

  • Crit chance = your Crit chance + the weapon's Crit chance.
  • Crit chance over 100% overcrits. The game rolls the number of crits: one guaranteed crit per full 100%, plus one more with the leftover chance. 230% = 2 crits, with a 30% chance of 3.
  • The multiplier grows faster than the crit count (it is (1 + crits/2)², except a single crit is ×2):
Crits on the hit 0 1 2 3 4 5
Multiplier ×1 ×2 ×4 ×6.25 ×9 ×12.25
  • That multiplier is then multiplied again by Crit damage (base ×1.0).
  • Average multiplier examples: 100% crit = ×2, 150% = ×3, 200% = ×4, 300% = ×6.25, 400% = ×9.
  • Takeaway: past 100%, every extra 100% crit chance is worth more than the last. Crit chance is the strongest scaling stat in the game for direct weapon hits.
  • Aegis adds your Thorns to its damage.
  • Execute: normal enemies that can be executed die outright; bosses and enemies that can't be executed take 2% of their max HP instead.

3. Item procs and the proc coefficient

  • Every on-hit item roll is chance × the weapon's proc coefficient (Sniper 1.5; Dice and most weapons 1.0; Katana 0.95; Axe, Aura, Black Hole, Dragon's Breath 0.7).
  • Damage from poison, burn ticks and similar effects has proc coefficient 0, so it never triggers on-hit items. There are no chain reactions off damage-over-time.
  • Elite damage multiplies all damage against elites, damage-over-time included.

4. Poison

  • Damage per tick = stacks × Damage × Poison damage, never less than 1 per stack.
  • 2 ticks per second for the duration × Duration multiplier.
  • No stack cap. Every application adds stacks. Re-applying only lengthens the timer if the new duration is longer than what's left.
  • Not affected by crits, weapon damage or boss damage. Reduced by enemy armor.

5. Burn, freeze, stun

  • Your base damage = 8 + your level. Burn and some items scale off this, not your weapon.
  • Burn: one hit per second of (8 + level) × Damage × 0.85, for 3 s × Duration. It doesn't stack; re-applying only refreshes it.
  • Freeze: 3 s; the game's freeze slow value is 0.3.
  • Stun (Lightning Orb): 3 s.
  • Dragonfire, Ice Cube and Lightning Orb each roll twice per hit: once for the status (burn/freeze/stun) and once for their extra effect (fire field, ice burst, lightning strike). Both rolls are scaled by proc coefficient.

6. Damage you take

Damage taken = enemy damage × (1 − armor reduction) × (1 − damage reduction), minimum 1.

  • Against boss attacks your armor counts half; against the final boss it counts 75%. Some attacks ignore armor.
  • Evasion is a chance to dodge a hit entirely.
  • Overheal cap = Max HP × Overheal.
  • Lifesteal works like crits: one guaranteed heal per full 100%, plus a chance for one more.

7. How enemies scale

Enemy HP = base HP × (1 + time + final swarm + stage + 1.1 × Difficulty) × type × your Enemy HP multiplier

Part Formula
Time +10% per minute of the run × Enemy scaling multiplier
Final swarm 4^(minutes into the final swarm) − 1
Stage Stage 1: +0. Stage 2: +27 × (1 + 0.45 × Difficulty). Stage 3: +145 × (1 + 0.45 × Difficulty)
Type Elite ×10, challenge ×7, elite challenge ×15; bosses ×2.5 on stage 2 and ×5 on stage 3

Enemy damage = base × (1 + 2.8% per minute + (2^(final swarm minutes) − 1) + stage bonus + Difficulty) × 1.5 for elites × your Enemy damage multiplier. The stage bonus looks like +2 on stage 2 and +4 on stage 3 (partly traced).

What this means:

  • Stage 2 enemies start at roughly 30× base HP, and stage 3 at roughly 150×, while stage 1 ends near 2×. Your damage has to grow about 15× from the end of stage 1 to the start of stage 2.
  • Final swarm HP multiplies by 4 every minute: +3 after one minute, +15 after two, +63 after three. Damage doubles every minute. Farming the swarm runs into a wall fast, so the kills come from killing huge numbers early in the swarm, not from surviving longer.
  • Difficulty adds directly to HP and damage, and stage HP scales with it too. Difficulty is a risk dial, not a free bonus.
  • Some special enemies have HP tied to your level: (level + 25) × 20, or level + 30.

8. Luck

  • Luck changes rarity weights for level-up offers, chests, Moai and Shady Guys.
  • Each rarity's weight is scaled by (1 + Luck)^(−0.61 × steps below the top rarity), then the weights are normalised.
  • So each step down in rarity loses about ×1.5 relative weight at Luck 100%, ×1.9 at 200% and ×2.3 at 300%: steady but diminishing returns. (This assumes the curve function is a logarithm, which its input checks suggest. The base weights themselves weren't recovered.)
  • Level-up offers then multiply the rolled value by the rarity multiplier (Common 1.0, Uncommon 1.2, Rare 1.4, Epic 1.6, Legendary 2.0).
